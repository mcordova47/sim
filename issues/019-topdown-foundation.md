## Goal
Switch the game from a side-view platformer to a top-down, Pokemon-style game: the player walks tile to tile, faces a direction and presses the interact key on whatever is in front of them. Keep everything that is not about the view (loop, input, camera, HUD, font, popups, scoring and combos, task events and layers, palette, sprite loader, tests). Replace the platformer-only parts. This is a real foundation, not a throwaway, and it ends in a decision gate: Marc plays it and says whether the direction feels right before the rest of the pivot (020 onward) starts.

## Acceptance criteria
- **Controls:** four directions (WASD or arrows) and interact (E). The jump controls go.
- **Grid steps:** the player always stands on a tile (16 px). Pressing a direction you are not facing first turns you to face it; holding it then steps. A step takes `stepTime` (about 0.16 s) with smooth movement between tiles for drawing. Holding a direction keeps stepping with no gap; changing direction mid-hold is handled at the next tile; no diagonal movement. Timings live in `config.ts`.
- **Collision:** tile-based, from the level's solid rectangles snapped to the grid (and the map edge). Walking into something is a bump: you stay put, and the bump is announced once, not every frame. No gravity.
- **Interaction target:** the player interacts with the object on the faced tile. `Interactable` zones become tile rectangles that can be used from an adjacent faced tile. `InteractionSystem` uses the faced tile and drops the "grounded" rule. The prompt appears when facing something you can use. The buffered press, cooldowns, blocked press, scoring, combos, popups and HUD keep working, with their tests adapted.
- **Events:** `jump` and `land` are replaced by `step` (the tile and direction) and `bump` (the blocked tile). `turn` is optional. Update the event types, tests and the notes in `features.ts`.
- **Camera:** the existing camera follows the player on maps bigger than the view and holds still on a map that fits. Whole-pixel drawing is kept.
- **A placeholder level:** one room of rectangles (walls, a few furniture blocks, three placeholder tasks such as fridge, sink and bed), the player as a rectangle with a facing marker. Draw order is by row (y) so tall things overlap correctly once there is art.
- **Remove what no longer applies:** the platformer physics and its tests, `reach.ts`, and the side-on floor-plan conversion and data. Nothing in the repo may be left dead or half-working. They stay in git history and the `side-view-prototype` tag. Side-view sprites may stay in `public/sprites/` until the art issues replace them, but nothing may depend on them.
- **Tests (no browser):** turn before step; a held key keeps stepping; direction change; blocked move bumps once; no diagonals; map edges; the faced tile is the target (facing away or diagonal does nothing); step and bump events fire once per tile or bump and never on idle frames; the camera still behaves; scoring and interaction behave as before.
- `npm run build` and `npm test` pass. A recording-canvas replay is not expected to match `main` (behaviour changes on purpose), but the HUD, popups and prompt draw calls should match for the same state.

## Decision gate
When this is done and reviewed, Marc plays it and says continue, adjust or stop. Do not start 020, 021, 022 or 023 until then. The interview (024), the art spec (025) and layer hardening (018) do not depend on it and can run now.

  - **Findings (none blocks approval), most likely to matter first:**
    1. Feel risk for the gate: changing direction while walking costs `turnTime` (0.08 s) at every tile boundary, because a new direction at arrival always turns first. A corner in a held path hitches. Also a perpendicular tap shorter than 0.08 s at a tile boundary turns the player and stops them. Pokemon only turns in place from a standstill.
    2. `Grid` is built once from `level.solids`. Issue 021 (apartment state: doors, piles that block) cannot change what is walkable without a way to rebuild or update the grid. `Game` also takes one level for life; doorways between rooms (a pillar in PLAN.md) need a level-switch API.
    3. Zones are the bounding rectangle of an object's tiles. An L-shaped object (the parser allows it) makes the empty notch tile usable: facing the notch from the floor starts the task. The real floor plan (020) will have L-shaped counters and sofas. Use `isOneObject` plus a rectangle check, or per-tile zones.
    4. Public surface for later issues: `col`/`row` are the tile being left while stepping; `target` is null mid-step; `step` fires on arrival (footsteps will lag 0.16 s); events carry no from-tile; `arrivedThisUpdate` is only the last arrival of a long update (a 0.25 s update can lose a step event and even a round trip). The game loop uses a fixed 1/60 step so this cannot happen today, but a direct `update(bigDt)` loses events. The busy lock exists only through `InteractionSystem.active`, so dialogs (023) and cutscenes (022) need their own way to freeze input and to move the player by script.
    5. `bumpReported` resets when a task locks the player (it receives NO_INPUT), so holding a direction into the object you just used bumps again after each task. At most one per task; probably fine, but sound hooks will notice.
    6. The prompt (`nearby`) lags the player by one tick (it is computed before the player moves), so it can show for one frame after a step begins. Same order as `main`. Harmless.
    7. Dead or stale leftovers against "nothing left dead": `README.md` still says "A single-room platformer" and `src/` layout is out of date; `src/game/tasks.ts` (`TASKS`) is used only by `ui-data.test.ts`, and the placeholder level defines its own tasks, so the label-width test checks data the game does not use (the placeholder prompts are only checked for font coverage); `src/render/sprites.ts` and `public/sprites/` are unused (declared by the author). `docs/floor-plan.md` body still names `room.ts` and `reach.ts` (it has the superseded note).
    8. tilemap: a legend entry for `#`, `.` or `@` is silently ignored (the built-in wins); a bad `color` name silently falls back to `mist` (type-only guard); CRLF input gives `Unknown character ""`; a non-string `rows` throws a raw TypeError; one object over about 125,000 tiles overflows the stack in `Math.min(...cols)`. `Level.spawn` and `Level.size` are not validated (a hand-written level with a spawn in a wall, or a size that is not a whole number of tiles, is accepted; a partial last tile is walkable).
    9. No culling in `drawWorld`: every floor tile and solid is a `fillRect` each frame (about 2,100 per frame on a 60x34 map, 90% off screen). Fine for one apartment room; revisit with art.
  - **Is `player.ts` too clever?** No. The `update` loop is about 40 lines and the carry-over of leftover time is what makes speed exact; my independent model matched it everywhere, including carry-over after turns and arrivals. It is mildly fragile in that facing, `turnTimer` and `bumpReported` all mutate inside the loop and the per-update flags only keep the last event, so anything that wants every event of a long update would need a list instead. Fine at a fixed 1/60 step.
